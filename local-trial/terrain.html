Using the 3D weather viewer

Explore the forecast across the landscape. This is an experimental model forecast; official MetService warnings take precedence.

Move around

Drag to rotate, scroll to zoom, and Shift-drag or right-drag to pan. On a touchscreen, drag with one finger to rotate, pinch to zoom and drag with two fingers to pan. Double-click with a mouse to focus. Reset view restores the region overview; Top-down view looks north-up from above. Choose a region under Explore.

Fly to a location

Search under Find a location. Choose Fly to & stay to hold a stationary view, or Fly to & orbit to circle. The camera turns towards the location, approaches, then starts a 60-second circuit, 100 km away and 5 km above sea level. Adjust the height, distance and circuit time beneath the location search while circling. Drag to take control, choose Resume orbit to continue, or use Reset view to return to the overview.

Terrain and wind trails

Detailed terrain loads finer Copernicus relief as you zoom, pan or orbit anywhere in New Zealand, down to about 90 m. The readout shows the finest grid currently visible. Switch it off to see model terrain. Weather uses the 0.1° IFS forecast grid. Terrain height starts at true scale (1×). Terrain height changes the landscape; Cloud height independently stretches model cloud heights above sea level. Terrain starts at 1× and cloud height at 5×. Cloud height cannot be lower than terrain height: increasing terrain raises clouds when needed. Wind trails show surface flow, with colour and thickness indicating speed. Density changes the number of trails; animation speed changes their motion on screen, not the forecast wind.

Record a video

Choose a frame and resolution in the bottom toolbar, then press Record. Stop opens a preview with options to view or save the clip. On iPhone or iPad, choose Save to Photos, then Save Video in the share sheet. The recording includes the forecast time and map labels.

Colour the landscape

Rain / snow shows three-hour mean rates, with snow expressed as liquid-water equivalent, not snow depth. Temperature shows 2 m air temperature. Wind speed shows 10 m wind in km/h. Terrain shows elevation alone. The opacity slider reveals terrain beneath rain/snow or wind, remembering separate settings for each (50% and 20%). Temperature stays fully coloured.

Falling rain and snow

You can switch falling rain and falling snow on independently. In wide, oblique views the viewer automatically emphasises distant precipitation. Use Rain splashes to show or hide ground impacts in these views. The view opens with terrain colouring, the information panel minimised and wind animation speed at the middle setting. Small rain streaks and varied snowflakes complete each fall in 1.5 seconds. Both use the same density curve based on their liquid-water rate, reaching maximum density at 10 mm/h. Light precipitation has a small minimum density. At shallow viewing angles, more ground fits into each pixel. The viewer draws fewer distant raindrops to compensate for this apparent crowding. Forecast rainfall rates stay the same. From above, rain appears as blue dots, splashes as expanding rings and snow as white flakes. They fall within 500 m of the displayed terrain, below the estimated cloud base. Snow uses the forecast snow-water amount and snow level. Rain and snow use IFS forecast amounts, independently of the cloud visualisation.

Pressure and 3D clouds

MSL pressure contours show sea-level pressure every 2 hPa, lightly smoothed for display. 3D clouds by humidity gives illustrative cloud volumes on pressure levels, not observed cloud. All levels are selected by default. The middle-cloud threshold defaults to 65%; low/high thresholds scale with it (78%/52%), bounded at 0–100%. Texture adds gentle shading without changing cloud coverage or opacity. Cloud density adjusts transparency in both modes, starting at 15%.

Forecast time

Use the timeline, play or ±3 h buttons to move through the forecast. Now selects the nearest available time. Rain and snow are average rates over the preceding three hours. Click the landscape for a readout. This private trial has no generated text forecasts.

Click the landscape or search for a place, then open Point forecast for hourly charts. Drag its heading to move it or its corners to resize. Generate text forecast summarises the remaining hours; it may queue or pause while the weather model runs. Official alerts are shown separately. Copy can include both text and the meteogram.

Other controls

Experimental fronts is a research overlay. Share this view copies the selected location/view; the link opens the latest forecast for that view. Minimise the panels or use Hide controls for a clear view.
